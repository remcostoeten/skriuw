Skiruw lets you publish a note as a public link so anyone can read it — no account, no invite, just a URL.

## How it works

Open a note and hit **Share**, then flip on **Make this note public**. Skiruw publishes a frozen snapshot of the note and gives you a link to copy and send. The person you share it with doesn't need a Skiruw account to open it — they just need the link.

## What recipients can do

Shared links are **view-only**. Anyone with the link can read the note exactly as it looked when you published (or last refreshed) it — they can't edit it, comment on it, or change anything. If you keep writing after sharing, the public page won't update until you refresh the snapshot.

## Controls you get

- **View once, then destroy** — the link works for exactly one view, then it's gone
- **Password protect** — require a password before the link opens
- **Show author** — credit yourself by name, or keep it anonymous
- **Expiry** — set the link to stop working after a duration or on a specific date
- **Unpublish anytime** — turn a note back to private with one click

## Keeping track

If the note changes after you've shared it, Skiruw flags the link as out of date so you can refresh the snapshot with one click. A shared notes overview shows every link you've published — active, expired, consumed, or revoked — along with view counts over time, so you always know what's out there and who's looked at it.

## Why use sharing?

Sometimes a note needs to leave your workspace — a plan for a client, notes for a friend, a doc for someone without an account. Sharing gets it out the door as a clean, read-only page in one click, with full control over how long it lasts and who can get in, without turning your note into something anyone else can edit.
