# Connect ideas without slowing down

> Draft feature-page copy for the proposed relationship system. Publish after the implementation and release gate are complete.

Skriuw keeps related work close at hand. Add a tag when you want to group notes, add a person when a note is about someone, and mention another note when one idea leads to another.

## Find a thread, not just a file

Type `#` to add a tag. Type `@` to mention a person or another note. Skriuw offers a short, keyboard-first list as you type, so you can keep writing instead of hunting through a sidebar.

Once you choose an item, Skriuw stores the relationship—not just the visible words. Renaming a note, person, or tag updates where it appears across your workspace. Your writing stays put.

## See what connects

Open a note to see its backlinks: the notes that point to it. Open a person or tag to see the work connected to it. These views are ready with your workspace, so opening a note never waits for a search or a sync request.

## Local by default

People are workspace contacts, not accounts. Tags, people, and mentions live in your local workspace and travel with a workspace export. There is no network lookup behind a keystroke.

## Built for the long workspace

The relationship system is designed around stable IDs and prebuilt local indexes. That means a renamed note does not trigger a rewrite of every document, and a large workspace does not turn completion or navigation into a loading state.

If an item is removed, Skriuw shows the reference clearly rather than quietly pointing it somewhere else. Your links remain understandable, even when the workspace changes.
